\begin{abstract}
The ARC WhiteBox Estimation Challenge asks for estimates of post-ReLU mean activations in random MLPs under standard Gaussian inputs, with a score that rewards both low final-layer error and low effective compute. This write-up describes a structure-aware, hybrid estimator built around three observations. First, analytically propagating means and variances using an isotropic Gaussian approximation can identify neurons as almost certainly inactive, almost certainly active, or uncertain given how likely their pre-activation values are to be near the ReLU kink. Second, expensive Monte Carlo samples are most valuable on the uncertain paths and on late-layer classifications whose sign errors propagate to the scored layer. Third, the sampled activation matrix is structurally sparse even after removing almost certainly dead neurons. Sorting columns by firing rate exposes a cold block in which each sample row activates only a handful of columns. Grouping rows by their nonzero count allows each group to multiply only the columns it actually uses, while rows with no activity in the cold block skip it entirely. These observations lead to an estimator that combines analytical moment propagation, targeted antithetic Sobol sampling, exact packed sparse multiplication for the cold block, and guarded Strassen multiplication for the dense block.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Note on AI usage.} This work was developed with heavy use of large language models. The investigation began with human-led exploration---visualizing the networks' behavior and posing questions about the patterns that emerged---and the resulting ideas were implemented in code by LLMs under our direction. An initial draft of this write-up was likewise produced using LLMs and then substantially revised by the authors.
\end{abstract}
