\begin{abstract}
The ARC WhiteBox Estimation Challenge asks for estimates of post-ReLU mean activations in random MLPs under standard Gaussian inputs, with a score that rewards both low final-layer error and low effective compute. This write-up describes a structure-aware, hybrid estimator built around three observations. First, analytically propagating means and variances using an isotropic Gaussian approximation can identify neurons as almost certainly inactive, almost certainly active, or uncertain given how likely their pre-activation values are to be near the ReLU kink. Second, expensive Monte Carlo samples are most valuable on the uncertain paths and on late-layer classifications whose sign errors propagate to the scored layer. Third, most zeros in the sampled activation matrix sit in a band of rarely-firing columns. If columns are sorted by firing rate, almost every sample row carries only a handful of nonzeros across that cold band; grouping rows by that count lets each group multiply only the few columns it actually fires on, while rows that never touch the band skip it entirely---all without changing the sampled estimator. These observations motivate an algorithm that combines analytical moment propagation, targeted antithetic Sobol sampling, and an exact packed-prefix multiplication on top of guarded Strassen multiplication for the dense block.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Note on AI usage.} This work was developed with heavy use of large language models. The investigation began with human-led exploration---visualizing the networks' behavior and posing questions about the patterns that emerged---and the resulting ideas were implemented in code by LLMs under our direction. An initial draft of this write-up was likewise produced using LLMs and then substantially revised by the authors.
\end{abstract}
