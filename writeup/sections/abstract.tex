\begin{abstract}
The ARC WhiteBox Estimation Challenge asks for estimates of post-ReLU mean activations in random MLPs under standard Gaussian inputs, with a score that rewards both low final-layer error and low effective compute. This write-up describes a structure-aware, hybrid estimator built around three observations. First, analytically propagating means and variances using an isotropic Gaussian approximation can identify neurons as almost certainly inactive, almost certainly active, or uncertain given how likely their pre-activation values are to be near the ReLU kink. Second, expensive Monte Carlo samples are most valuable on the uncertain paths and on late-layer classifications whose sign errors propagate to the scored layer. Third, the matrix of sampled activations for a given layer is sparse, and the zeros can be omitted from the matrix multiplications that propagate those activations to the next layer. These observations motivate an algorithm that combines analytical moment propagation, targeted antithetic Sobol sampling, and cost-aware routing between sparse and dense matrix multiplication kernels.

\noindent\textit{Note on AI usage.} This work was developed in close collaboration with large language models. An initial draft of this write-up was likewise produced by an LLM and then substantially revised by the authors.
\end{abstract}
