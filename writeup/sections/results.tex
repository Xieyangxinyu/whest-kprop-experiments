\section{Evidence and Ablations}

\subsection{Public Evaluator Result}

\Cref{tab:public-result} reports the public Phase 1 evaluator result for the submitted artifact and the immediately preceding submitted baseline. The final method improved the adjusted score by $9.0408\%$ while preserving raw final-layer MSE at the reported precision. The gain came almost entirely from the compute multiplier, consistent with the goal of changing how the same sampled computation is routed rather than changing the prediction target.

\subsection{Local Gates Used Before Submission}

The public-mini subprocess checks used fixed seed 42 and profiled the same estimator surface before submission. The dense fallback for high-fire rows improved adjusted score by $12.6\%$ on three MLPs and $13.9\%$ on five MLPs with unchanged raw MSE. The cap-sample wall-time check also timed all public-mini MLPs that reached the sample cap and found no local 60-second failures; the maximum direct wall time among those cap rows was $25.026$s.

These checks were not treated as definitive public scores. Their purpose was narrower: reject variants that changed raw MSE, exceeded wall time, or only improved a noisy local timing signal without reducing tracked compute.

\subsection{Negative Results}

Several plausible extensions were rejected. Packing only pilot/probe matmuls preserved raw final MSE but touched too small a fraction of compute to pay for its overhead. More aggressive approximate compression variants changed the prediction enough that raw final-layer MSE dominated any compute savings on the tested slices. Naive sparse-support variants exposed real support overlap, but were too slow and timed out or regressed on public-mini checks.

These failures are useful because they separate two kinds of progress. Exact routing changes that preserve predictions and reduce the multiplier transfer well. Approximate packing changes need a raw-MSE error below the remaining multiplier gain; otherwise, they lose even when they save counted FLOPs.
